# Four-faction strategy game — design brief and day-one plan

Version 0.1 • 25 September 2026 • Owner: Linus Floyd

## Direction

A PC turn-based strategy game on a hex map, with 3D presentation, city progression, four asymmetric living factions, AI opponents, cooperative teams, and PvP. Living factions can use crewed vehicles. Faction names are working names. This brief incorporates the accepted revisions to F1.txt; that source file remains the original reference. New precise rules below are prototype defaults, not tested balance conclusions.

The strategic contrast is: biological timing, industrial preparation, elite commitment, and deceptive reconnaissance. All factions need meaningful scouting, defense, expansion, and a way to defeat cities. Their advantages must have readable counterplay.

| Faction | Main decision | Economy/settlement identity | Main vulnerability |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 — The Living Migration | When and where to turn terrain into military strength | Mobile spider headquarters; three recruitment life cycles | Connection disruption, food pressure, mistimed development |
| F2 — Baumeister | Where to build the next protected route | Permanent pillar cities linked by bridges | Exposed extensions, severed supply, slow repositioning |
| F3 — Votive Houses | Which objective deserves scarce veterans? | A few citadels, house specialization, public vows | Low numbers, replacement costs, competing fronts |
| F4 — Veilkin | What should the enemy believe before an attack? | Fixed concealed settlements, intelligence economy | Detection, sustained combat, reliable reconnaissance |

## Shared foundations

- Logical axial hex coordinates (q, r), independent of models and physics.
- One military unit per hex initially. Roads, bridges, resources, and districts are tile/edge properties. Multi-unit transport is a later system.
- Sequential player turns. Team membership is independent of faction and controller type.
- Full game: team-shared vision and team victory; resource gifting is deferred until its rules are designed.
- Server/host validates every move, attack, recruitment, construction, and end-turn command.
- AI and humans use the same legal commands. AI sees permitted observations, not the full hidden world.
- Prototype victory: eliminate all hostile headquarters; surviving members of the last team win.
- Damage previews, movement highlights, readable status durations, and explicit rejected-command feedback.
- Stats and costs belong in data definitions. Visual nodes do not own gameplay state.

## F1 — The Living Migration

### Identity

A symbiotic ecosystem of spiders, ants, amphibians, and flying insects. Its units represent different investment timelines. Its settlements travel, but movement creates operational trade-offs.

### Economy and command

- Food pays unit upkeep and recruitment costs where applicable.
- Silk funds spider headquarters and specialist/hero upkeep. Spider costs increase with headquarters count; each spider also produces silk.
- Synapse is a stored recruitment currency generated by synaptic creatures. It is distinct from Connection, a positional condition.
- Connected ant units are within a queen/hill's influence or on an active ant-road connection. After two disconnected turns, they cannot accept offensive orders and attempt a legal retreat toward a connection. If no route exists, they hold. Show the countdown.
- Brood units are immune to morale penalties. Spider headquarters ignore the Baumeister structural Integrity system.

### Spider headquarters

Nine internal building slots, rather than nine additional world hexes. Each spider chooses one nursery branch: ant hill, marsh, or caterpillar foliage. Other slots support research, clearing, food, and synapse production.

Retain four movement points as a starting value, subject to terrain and balance. A spider harvests when ending movement on eligible fresh terrain. Proposed movement trade-off: research continues, but construction and recruitment do not advance on a turn in which the spider moved. A spider can produce another spider.

Mite production uses one consistent, visible interval for all spiders, adjustable through upgrades. Remove the original hidden dependence on creation order.

Terrain has explicit fresh/depleted/regrowing states. Repeated clearing grants no repeated harvest until regrowth. Renewable baseline food production must exist so long games remain viable. Exact yields and regrowth timing need testing.

### Recruitment branches

| Branch | Rules | Units retained from original concept |
|---|---|---|
| Ant hill | Nine storage slots; persistent units; queen/road dependence; workers extend roads; eligible corpses can be consumed once for food | Arbeiterameise, Kämpferameise, Feuerameise, Ameisenkönigin hero |
| Marsh | Nine larval slots; larvae mature and may be held; deploying chooses the unlocked form; deployed units have visible remaining lifespans | Libelle, Mücke, Pfeilgiftfrosch, Ochsenfrosch, Unke hero |
| Caterpillar foliage | Caterpillars gather nutrients from eligible terrain and transform after meeting nutrient and time requirements; high food upkeep | Zitronenfalter, Feuerfuchs, Admiral hero |

Queens and worker ants retain road-dependent movement as the full-design starting point; initial emergence must place them on a valid road. Workers may extend up to three contiguous road hexes per turn from a legal connection, subject to costs. A moving hill's local connection moves with it; abandoned roads remain but require a valid source to stay active. Corpse food uses visible unit value and cannot be collected twice.

### Diseases

Mites are synaptic support units. They may change their selected disease instead of moving. Exposure is resolved at a defined turn boundary, initially the end of the mite owner's turn, against adjacent eligible enemies.

First set: Tracking (reveals the affected unit and a one-hex neighborhood), Weakening (temporary damage reduction), and Wasting (damage over time). One disease per target. Each has an explicit duration, followed by temporary immunity to that disease only. Research unlocks disease choices, not bypasses to unreadable immunity rules. Most brood creatures are immune; the original mite exception remains a later tuning question.

Resurrection, movement/armor debuffs, and structural infection remain future content. Any structural infection must be temporary or repairable; remove permanent unavoidable city penalties.

### Strength, weakness, cooperation

Prepare waves, protect developing organisms, consume useful territory, and maintain connections. Opponents punish disconnected ants, vulnerable nurseries, and poorly timed deployments. In co-op, ants hold routes while temporary units support an allied offensive.

## F2 — Baumeister

### Identity

A biological industrial civilization living in inhabited viaducts. Crewed armor, artillery, engineers, and infantry advance along prepared routes. Species and visual culture remain open.

### Cities and bridges

Builders create a pillar within a bounded connection distance of the existing network, then complete its bridge. Each pillar is a small city with access to up to six surrounding district hexes. A district belongs to one pillar only. The initial pillar can be founded independently.

Bridges are map-edge connections in the first implementation; there is no separately occupiable upper and lower layer. Connected cities offer supply, repair, and movement benefits. Destroying a span removes those network benefits from isolated pillars, not their existence or ordinary local production. Clearly telegraph construction and supply status.

Pillar artillery is a later defensive upgrade. Bridge benefits must help advancement, not make an unassailable artillery wall.

### Economy

| Resource/stat | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Food | Population and biological personnel upkeep |
| Materials | Buildings, vehicles, repairs, bridge maintenance |
| Energy | Advanced recruitment and selected abilities |
| Unity (Einheit) | Heroes and limited empire directives |
| Integrity | Local pillar condition, lost to damage or neglected maintenance and restored by repair |

Remove automatic global Integrity loss based on city number. Expansion instead costs materials, maintenance, build time, and defensive attention. Define local damage thresholds before implementing Integrity effects.

Districts: mines, greenhouses, power plants, civic monuments/shrines, barracks, hospitals, workshops. Their distinct output or unlock must be explicit; merge buildings that duplicate a role.

### Army

| Unit | Role |
|---|---|
| Entdeckersquad / Scout Trike | Reconnaissance and target spotting; begin with one, differentiate later |
| Eroberersquad | Capture and hold territory |
| Bridgewrights | Build and repair bridges and structures; field repair |
| Transportpanzer | Move vulnerable infantry; deferred until transport rules exist |
| Assaultpanzer | Armored frontline protection |
| Long Range Panzer | Deployable indirect fire with repositioning cost |
| Flugabwehrpanzer | Air defense |
| Medicsquad | Heal biological personnel, not vehicles |
| Trike Commander | Hero improving coordination and target designation |

Panzersquad, Deathsquad, Botschafter, and Aufseher remain reserve concepts until they have unique jobs.

### Strength, weakness, cooperation

Spot, deploy, protect, repair. Opponents cut supply or outmaneuver the firing line. Allies benefit from defended staging positions; shared repair/access rules must be explicit and bounded.

## F3 — Votive Houses

### Identity

Long-lived horned beings, organized into houses, with elite warriors, retainers, banners, and trained war beasts. A small army with high investment per unit.

### Economy and cities

Food and ordinary production sustain citadels and armies. A small number of fixed citadels select house specializations. Renown, earned through public vows, unlocks branching development and capped specialization rather than unlimited combat bonuses.

### Signature mechanic: vows

Commit an army to a public objective for a fixed window: hold a location, capture a district, escort a unit, or defend an allied city. Opponents can inspect the objective and countdown. Completion grants Renown; failure temporarily restricts new vows. Begin with one active vow, limited eligible objectives, and a cooldown to prevent farming trivial tasks. Ordinary economic development must remain possible without successful vows.

### Proposed roster

- Hearthguard: durable line infantry.
- Lancer Riders: expensive beast cavalry for decisive attacks.
- Oathblade: duelist that threatens exposed high-value units.
- Bannerkeeper: support with a limited protection/rally ability.
- Greatbow Retainers: limited ranged support.
- Gatebreaker: slow siege beast providing a reliable city-assault option.
- House Scion: hero specializing veteran units, with capped effects.

### Strength, weakness, cooperation

Preserve veterans and select commitments carefully. Enemies deny vows, attack another front, or trade cheap troops for irreplaceable elites. Allied-defense vows provide a natural co-op role. Avoid permanent death spirals from failed vows and excessive rewards for already winning.

## F4 — Veilkin

### Identity

Living chromatophoric beings communicating through color and vibration. Concealment, false contacts, reconnaissance, and prepared engagements define their warfare.

### Economy and cities

Fixed settlements specialize in recruitment, intelligence, or trade. Normal resources pay upkeep and construction. Intelligence funds limited deception abilities and is generated through bounded reconnaissance rewards; repeatedly observing the same contact cannot produce unlimited income.

### Signature mechanic: deception

False unit contacts mislead enemy reconnaissance. Close inspection or an attack reveals a decoy. Decoys cannot block movement, capture, damage, or act as real scouting units. Concealment depends on terrain or an explicit ability, with reliable detection tools available to every faction. Attacking reveals the attacker. Concealed settlements become discoverable on close approach and remain recorded as last-known sites.

Separate authoritative reality from each team's observed contacts. AI must respond to the same observations as human opponents. Basic hiding precedes decoys in implementation order.

### Proposed roster

- Colorwatcher: scout and detector.
- Veilstalker: infiltrator and ambush attacker.
- Dart Hunter: fragile short-range damage dealer.
- Mirrorweaver: support creating decoys and obscuring selected movement.
- Shellwarden: dependable defensive core.
- Breach Hunter: limited siege specialist, vulnerable in prolonged exchanges.
- Manyface: hero improving deception options, not permanent invisibility.

### Strength, weakness, cooperation

Set up favorable fights and break contact. Enemies use overlapping reconnaissance, defended objectives, and sustained pressure. Allies exploit information and prepared ambushes. PvP readability is essential: visible clues, clear reveal conditions, and bounded ability durations.

## Tonight's deliverable

Target: a runnable Windows prototype with a complete small match, not four implemented factions. One 10×10 handcrafted hex map; two sides using the same temporary Baumeister-derived roster; three unit types; one headquarters per side; one material currency; recruitment; deterministic combat; sequential turns; basic AI; defeat/victory; restart. Colored primitive 3D models and a top-down camera are sufficient.

Start with full map visibility. Real fog of war, faction powers, research trees, city districts, transport, saving/reconnection, procedural maps, audio, polished art, and online services are later milestones. Today’s networking, if reached, is a full-visibility development mode, not competitive-ready multiplayer.

### Starter rules to prevent design delays

- Map: 10×10 valid axial coordinates, all passable initially.
- Move once and attack once per turn; moving after attacking is disallowed. A move follows an unobstructed route no longer than movement allowance.
- Infantry: 10 HP, move 2, range 1, damage 3, cost 4.
- Scout: 6 HP, move 3, range 1, damage 2, cost 3.
- Gunner: 7 HP, move 1, range 2, damage 3, cost 5.
- Headquarters: 25 HP, no attack. Each player starts with 8 materials and gains 3 at their turn start.
- Recruitment places one unit on a free neighboring hex and deducts cost exactly once. New units cannot act until their next turn. At most one recruitment per headquarters per turn.
- No armor, random damage, line-of-sight obstacles, retaliation, or faction bonuses tonight. Attack range uses hex distance.
- Start with one infantry and one scout per side. Destroy the opposing headquarters to win. Stop input after victory except restart.
- AI recruits when possible, attacks a legal target, otherwise approaches the enemy headquarters via legal paths, and always finishes its turn. It must terminate when blocked.

## Recommended development setup

Primary: local Windows, Godot 4 standard (GDScript), Compatibility renderer, VS Code or an existing AI-capable editor, and Git. Install matching export templates early. Use Godot’s own editor for scenes, imports, inspection, and running the game. Use the code editor/agent for scripts and reviewable changes. No paid editor is required to use this plan.

Keep the first project structure small: project.godot; scenes/main.tscn; scripts/core/{hex_grid,game_state,rules}.gd; scripts/controllers/{human_controller,ai_controller}.gd; scripts/view/board_view.gd; data/units.json; docs/brief.md; tests/smoke.gd. Add scripts/net only when networking begins. Names are a proposed layout, not files already implemented by this brief.

Git: commit after each acceptance gate. Ignore .godot/ and exported builds; keep source scenes, scripts, project settings, data, and Godot UID files. Add Git LFS when large binary art arrives. Do not spend today setting up Docker, a backend, Kubernetes, matchmaking, or a broad CI pipeline.

Codespaces: suitable for editing, headless rule checks, and exporting once a pinned Godot binary and matching templates are installed. It is a Linux container, not a Windows graphical development desktop. Browser preview is possible by creating a Godot web export and serving it through a forwarded HTTP port. Use Compatibility rendering and GDScript; native ENet/UDP multiplayer is not equivalent to browser multiplayer. A private Codespaces web preview is not a game-hosting service. Recommendation: local Godot today; optional Codespaces later for portable editing and checks.
